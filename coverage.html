Search coverage, empty searches and access limits

Discovery and currency were handled separately. Sources were searched to add resources and source varieties, not to demand corroboration before inclusion. Relevant single-source, historical and uncertain leads remain in the directory. Unrelated search hits, commercial services with no discernible resource connection, and duplicate names for the same program were not silently converted into new aid providers.

This is a substantial public-source directory, not a claim that the entire neighborhood ecosystem has stopped growing. Repeated public searches produced many repeats and unrelated hits, while targeted searches still occasionally added a lead. True saturation cannot be established for private social groups or offline networks without community access. Coverage depth and currency are reported separately below.

What remains thin

Private WhatsApp/Telegram/Nextdoor/Facebook groups; current hometown-club leadership; named tienda, laundromat and barbershop bulletin hosts; undocumented worker-to-worker hiring referrals; a current Armourdale tenant union; and pantry schedules available only by phone or church bulletin. No people were contacted, no private groups joined, and no personal resident contact details were collected. ARA, promotoras, the school family liaison, CHC and the Consulate’s community-affairs desk are concrete entry points for extending those searches with community participation.

Not established: a currently operating Armourdale community fridge, a currently open Armourdale-only flood relief fund, or an operating farmers’ market arising from the plan’s proposals. These gaps do not remove broader services or historical leads already found.
